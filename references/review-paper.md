# Review-paper reading guide

Use this guide for narrative reviews, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses. Identify the subtype; their evidential strength and structure differ.

## Chunk 1 — Content Analysis

- State the review's stated rationale, purpose, theoretical or practical context, and scope.
- Map each main heading/subheading to its central issue, evidence base as described by the authors, and conclusion. Preserve disagreement and uncertainty rather than flattening the field into a consensus.
- For systematic reviews/meta-analyses, report available search, inclusion, coding, bias/risk-of-bias, and synthesis information. Distinguish reviewed primary studies from the review authors' conclusions.
- End with the review's stated value, limitations, and implications, anchoring each to the paper.
- For terms, theories, regions, and components, use Chinese plus English full name/abbreviation on first occurrence when available.

## Chunk 2 — Academic Language

Prioritize language for synthesizing evidence, contrasting findings, expressing uncertainty, identifying gaps, and proposing future directions. Use the same source-derived pattern, reusable template, Chinese explanation, and safe example structure described in the empirical-paper guide.

## Chunk 3 — Citation Traceability

Clarify the chain: review statement -> in-text citation(s) -> reference-list entry. A citation in a review is evidence that the review attributes a claim, not proof that every cited primary study supports the statement.

## Chunk 4 — Critical Reading

Separate review-internal constraints (scope, search, inclusion, heterogeneity, bias assessment, synthesis) from additional methodological suggestions. Further innovation must follow the external-search authorization rule.
